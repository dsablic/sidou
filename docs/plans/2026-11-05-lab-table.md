# A step table on a LAB sound: design

**Status:** design for 1.7, 2026-11-05.

## Why

SidStation patches get much of their character from a step table (a waveform and pitch program
per oscillator). 1.6 imports them as GoatTracker wave programs on extra song instruments, but the
LAB sound itself has no table, so pulling an imported patch into LAB loses it, and a keyboard
sound cannot do the classic C64 tricks (a noise attack into a pulse, an octave arpeggio, a drum
that drops in pitch). Of the 90 factory patches, 13 sound a table (on 19 oscillators): 11 of the
19 have at most 16 steps, the longest 18; 9 of the 13 have it on their first oscillator.

## What the player does

A ninth LAB page, **TABLE**, after LAYERS. The table belongs to the sound: kept, compared, undone
and sent with it.

| Control | Does |
|---|---|
| ALGORITHM | `TABLE OFF`, `PLAY ONCE` (the last step holds), `LOOP FROM 1` .. `LOOP FROM n`; turned on from off on a sound with no steps, step 1 is the layer's own waveform at +0, so it is heard at once and changes nothing until edited |
| K1 STEP | the step the other cards edit, `1` .. the table's length, then `NEW` (one past the end, up to 16): a WAVE or NOTE turn there adds the step, a copy of the one before |
| K2 WAVE | the step's waveform: `END` (counter-clockwise from the first: the table ends before this step), `OFF` (no waveform: the step is silent), `TRI`, `SAW`, `PUL`, `NOI`, `T+S`, `T+P`, `S+P`, `TSP`, `TRR` (tri ring), `SAS` (saw sync), `PUS` (pulse sync); an imported control outside the list shows as its hex (`88`) and turns to the nearest |
| K3 NOTE | the step's pitch: `-24` .. `+24` semitones from the key; an imported absolute note shows its name (`C-4`) and turns a semitone a detent |
| K4 RATE | frames of 50 Hz a step, `1` .. `16` (`2FR`), one rate for the whole table, as the SidStation's table speed |

The drawing shows the steps as columns: each step's pitch as a bar from the middle line, its
waveform's colour, the edited step lit, the loop's start marked, and a dot on the step the last
note plays. The header says `TABLE L2` when the table plays on a layer other than the first.

Which voices play it: on a one-oscillator sound every voice runs the table from its own note-on
(a chord plays it on each note); on a layered sound it plays on one layer, the layer LAYERS' EDIT
picked when the table was turned on (stored with the sound). The table replaces that layer's
waveform each step (the gate stays the key's) and adds its note to the key's.

Every card value is at most three characters, at 2x or 3x, so LAB's rule holds: the card lights
instead of a popup (NO_POPUP's LAB row).

## Sound format

A sound grows from 68 to 103 bytes (`LAB_SOUND_BYTES_1_6` = 68 is kept as the old size):

| Offset | Bytes | Value |
|---|---|---|
| 68 | 1 | Table: bit 7 on, bits 5-6 the layer it plays on (`0..2`), bits 0-4 its steps (`0..16`; on needs at least 1) |
| 69 | 1 | Table loop: `0` plays once and holds the last step, `n` (`1..steps`) jumps back to step n after the last |
| 70 | 1 | Table rate: frames of 50 Hz a step, minus one, `0..15` |
| 71 | 32 | 16 steps of 2 bytes: the control (as `$d404` without the gate: the waveform, ring, sync and test bits) and the note (GoatTracker's wave-table right byte: `$00..$5F` up, `$60..$7F` down, `$80` none, `$81..$DF` an absolute note) |

A missing tail is the defaults, so 1.0, 1.1-1.5 and 1.6 sounds (45, 48, 68 bytes) load with the
table off. The USER sounds file's header says 103; the unsaved edit's record is 119 bytes. The
LBnn chunk grows to 9 + 103 bytes (16 chunks: 1,792 of the trailer area's 4,096). A 1.6 firmware
reads the first 68 bytes of a 1.7 sound and plays it without the table.

## The engine

`live_tick` runs at 100 Hz; the table steps every two ticks (a GoatTracker frame), per voice: a
position and a wait. Note-on starts the voice's table at step 1 at once. GoatTracker's own player
(`gt2_play.c`) is in RAMTEXT, which has 16 bytes free, so the table is not run by it; the table's
rows are GoatTracker's encoding (the same control and right bytes), so it maps one to one onto a
GoatTracker wave program both ways:

- **Send (REC):** the table becomes the instrument's wave program: each step a row, then a delay
  row of the rate's frames minus two (GoatTracker holds a delay row its value plus one frame; a
  `$00 $80` row for two frames), and a jump to the loop's step (or to the last
  step, a hold). Up to 33 rows. With ARP on, the arpeggio's program is sent as before and the
  table is reported dropped (`NOT SENT: TABLE`).
- **Pull:** a wave program that is not an arpeggio and has no wave commands is read back as a
  table when its delays are even (the first step's delay is the rate; others are approximated, `~WAVE`), up to 16 steps,
  instead of 1.6's `~DRUM`.
- **Import:** `sidou-patch` puts the first sounding oscillator's table on the LAB sound (on its
  layer), so the pulled sound keeps it; tables on the other layers still play from their song
  instruments, and the report says so (`OSC 2 TABLE: SONG INSTRUMENT`); a table over 16 steps
  is cut (`OSC 1 TABLE CUT TO 16`); the table's speed is converted to 50 Hz frames as 1.6 did.

## Budgets (estimate, measured at the end)

- Flash: about 3 KB (engine 0.5, page and drawing 1.5, send and pull 0.8, checks 0.2).
- RAM (`.bss`): about 450 bytes: the sound's 35 bytes in the live state, its base and USER
  edit copies, five preset edits, LAB's A copy and the store's record buffer; the undo ring
  grows from 72 to 104 entries a side (a step must fit); 6 bytes of play state.
- RAMTEXT: nothing (the engine is in `live.c`, from flash).
- Audio path: nothing; `live_tick` gains two compares a voice when no table is on.
