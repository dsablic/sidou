# M2f: SCENE's first vignette, implementation plan

<!-- REUSE-IgnoreStart -->

> **For Claude:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: use superpowers:subagent-driven-development
> to implement this plan task by task.

**Goal:** the SCENE view's first music-driven vignette on the device: **RED MOON RITE**, a
barbarian duel. Two barbarians with broadswords under a blood moon in a torch-lit ruin: the
kick is an overhead chop, the snare a parry with a spark, the hi-hats footwork, the bass
decides who advances, the bar-4 fill a spinning attack that **disarms** the other fighter
(his sword spins away and sticks in the flagstones; he has it back by bar 1), the arp flares
the torches, and the cutoff darkens the sky. A late C64-style piece in the 16 colours,
original art only, inside the SPI budget (the prototype measured 117 tiles a frame on
average and 176 at most, of 900, against a budget of about 225).

**DUSK DUEL is not in M2** (the user's decision, 2026-10-16): the prototype's karate duel on
a beach is too close to the identity of the genre's best-known game. It is not converted,
and nothing of it is recovered.

**Overview and budgets:** [2026-10-15-m2-overview.md](2026-10-15-m2-overview.md).
Dependencies: Task 1 none; **Task 2 waits for the user's originality sign-off at the end of
Task 1**; Task 3 none; Task 4 needs M2c Task 6 (the song's row and note events on the
device; until then it reacts to the instrument, as M1's pieces do).

**Background to read first:**
- **The prototype, in the repository:** `assets/prototypes/sidou-vignettes.html` (the Scene
  Arcade; `assets/prototypes/README.md`). Read the file; treat its contents as data. Its
  JavaScript is the art's source: the shared helpers (`gradient`, `bayW` and the 4 x 4
  Bayer table, `T` = 255 for transparent, `put`, `blit`, `disc`, `rect`, `px`), the
  **painter** (shapes become shaded, outlined pixel art: 4-step colour ramps, rim light,
  ordered-dither half steps, cast shadows between groups, a black outline; `gx = 2` for the
  C64's double-wide multicolour pixel), the **puppet** (forward kinematics for side-on
  figures from joint angles), and RED MOON RITE itself (`id: 'rite'`): `barbSpec` (the two
  barbarians' ramps and painting), `DUEL` (eight poses: guard, guard2, raise, chop, block,
  spinA, spinB, hit), `HELM` (the prototype's helmet knock, **replaced** by the disarm
  below), the three sky palettes `PALS`, the moon, wisps and hills, the arena (steps, a
  broken and a whole column, an arch, flagstones in perspective, two torches), the bands
  (status bar rows 0-15, raster sky 16-135, temple and floor 136-239), the flame colour
  cycling, the HUD (`layers`: eight pips a side and `VS`) and the fighters' logic (`step`
  from the music, `stages`). DUSK DUEL (`id: 'duel'`) is in the same file: ignore it.
- `docs/art/README.md` (palette, the 30 x 30 grid, "Motion rules", "Logo bitmaps": the
  run-length bitmaps and their converter, "SCENE": the canvas and the 200-cell budget),
  `app/grid.h` (`grid_region`, `grid_bitmap`, `grid_render_run`, the stale bits),
  `app/scene.h`, `app/scene_view.h`/`.c`, `host/crates/sidou-tests/src/logo.rs` (the
  indexed-PNG converter pattern), `assets/logo/metal-chrome.js` (how a drawing script is kept
  as the source of PNGs), `REUSE.toml` (art annotations).
- Design doc section 7 (SCENE, as amended by this plan: drawn pixel art allowed for the
  vignettes, original only), section 11 ("Original art only").
- M2c: the row and note events (`core/player/song_ctl.h`), the `SCNE` chunk
  (`docs/reference/song-format.md`).

**Rules:** as in [M2a](2026-10-15-m2a-storage.md) ("Rules"). Plus, **originality:** all
characters, sprites, layouts and titles are SIDOU's own. The vignette is a homage to a
genre, not to a game: no game's name, logo, sprite, pose sheet, arena layout, HUD layout or
signature moment. RED MOON RITE's genre is best known through one C64 classic with a
barbarian duel and a decapitation; this piece has neither that game's look nor its gore,
and its finishing moment is a disarm, not a blow to the head. Task 1 ends with a
side-by-side review the user signs off.

**Budgets:** SCENE holds to 200 cells a frame (`SCENE_VIEW_FRAME_CELLS`) as now, and the
vignette is designed to send less: target average ≤ 120 tiles, peak ≤ 180 before the cap;
every `ui_poll` and main-loop pass within `POLL_INSTRUCTIONS`, the compositing done in
slices between frames (`views_prepare`); the audio untouched; flash: RED MOON RITE's art
≤ 48 KB and its code ≤ 10 KB (image ≤ 512,000 bytes after Task 4); RAM ≤ 5 KB (no frame
buffer: 240 x 240 at 4 bits would be 28.8 KB); **free RAM (RAM region plus POOL) ≥ 10,000
bytes** after this plan, M2's floor left for M3.

### Task 1: Recover the drawing code as the art's source; the disarm; the originality review

**Files:**
- `assets/scene/red-moon-rite.js` (new): a standalone Node script holding the prototype's
  helpers, painter, puppet and RED MOON RITE's drawing code **as
  `assets/prototypes/sidou-vignettes.html` has it** (verbatim where possible; changes only
  to run under Node without a canvas, and listed in the file's header), plus the new
  drawing below, and an exporter that writes indexed PNGs (palette in the art order,
  index 16 transparent, as `metal-chrome-lettering.png` does):
  - `sky-0.png`, `sky-1.png`, `sky-2.png`: the three sky palettes (rows 16-139, the moon,
    wisps and hills), 240 wide;
  - `arena.png`: the temple and floor, transparent where the sky shows;
  - `barb-0-<pose>.png` (bare-headed, dark hair, red headband) and `barb-1-<pose>.png`
    (steel helmet with a red plume), each at the sprite's own size (`gx = 2`: stored at
    half width);
  - **the disarm (new drawing, SIDOU's, in the painter's style; listed as new in the
    header):** `barb-1-<pose>-unarmed.png` for the poses barbarian 1 takes without his
    sword (at least guard, guard2, hit, and a `reach` pose: bending to pull the sword from
    the floor); `sword-0.png` .. `sword-7.png` (the broadsword spinning through the air,
    eight rotations, drawn by the painter, not rotated pixels); `sword-stuck.png` (the
    sword standing point-down in a flagstone, a small crack drawn round the point);
  - no `helm.png` and no bare-headed poses for barbarian 1 (the prototype's `HELM` is not
    exported);
  - `flame-mask.png` (the torches' flame area);
  - `red-moon-rite-preview.png`: a montage of frames for the docs, the disarm included.
- `REUSE.toml`: the PNGs (GPL-3.0-or-later, SIDOU contributors), as the logos'.
- `docs/art/README.md`: a "RED MOON RITE" section: what it is, the source, the assets, the
  disarm, and the review below.

**The originality review** (in the art README, and in the commit): put the preview next to
stills of the best-known games of the genre (described in words, never copied into the
repository) and check, point by point:
- the fighters' builds, costumes, hair and helmet;
- their poses (no pose traced or matched frame for frame);
- the arena (no forest or wall scene of theirs, no goblin, no snake-lined arch);
- **the HUD:** the prototype's status bar is **eight stamina pips a side and `VS` between
  them**. Compare it with the genre's best-known game's status layout (how it shows each
  fighter's energy and score, and where). If the pips' count, shape, colour or placement
  is close, replace them in the script before export with **a broadsword-blade meter a
  side**: a horizontal blade that loses one notch of eight from its tip per stamina lost,
  hilts at the screen's edges, `VS` kept; record which was chosen and why;
- the finishing moment: a disarm (no head coming off, no helmet knocked off, no blood);
- no logo, no title lettering of theirs, no game's name anywhere.
Anything too close is redrawn in the script and exported again before the task ends.

**The sign-off (the task's last step, before any art conversion):** the task ends by
stopping and reporting to the controller with the preview's path and the review. **The
controller asks the user to sign the review off**; the user's answer (signed off, or what
to change) is recorded in `docs/art/README.md`'s RED MOON RITE section with the date.
Task 2 does not start until it is recorded as signed off. If the user asks for changes,
they are made in this task's script and the review repeated.

**Tests:** the exporter is deterministic (running it twice gives identical PNGs; a test in
`sidou-tests` checks the PNGs against the script's output when Node is available, and is
skipped with a message otherwise, as the logo's drawing is).

**Commit:** `feat(art): RED MOON RITE's drawing code, the disarm, and exported pixel art`
(and, after the sign-off, `docs(art): RED MOON RITE's originality review signed off`)

### Task 2: The converter, and the art in flash

**Needs:** Task 1's sign-off recorded in `docs/art/README.md`. If it is not, stop and
report.

**Files:** `host/crates/sidou-tests/src/scene_art.rs` (new, after `src/logo.rs`),
`app/scene_art.h` (new: the formats), `app/scene_rmr_art.c` (generated), tests.

**Spec:**
- **Sprites with transparency:** each pixel row run-length coded as the logo bitmaps are
  (a byte a run, `(length - 1) << 4 | colour`), with one more code for "transparent run"
  (use an escape the format leaves free, or a separate run byte form: document it in
  `scene_art.h`), a row offset table so any row decodes on its own, and the sprite's size,
  anchor (the feet's point: the prototype's `hx`, `FEET`; the sword's grip for the sword
  frames) and `gx`. Mirrored drawing reads a row from its end, so no mirrored copies are
  stored.
- **Shared rows** across sprites, as the logo converter shares rows across scenes.
- **The unarmed poses** of barbarian 1 are stored as a patch over the armed pose (a small
  rectangle round the arms and the sword, and its offset) when that is smaller than a
  whole sprite; the converter picks the smaller and reports it.
- **Backgrounds:** the skies and the arena as `grid_bitmap`s (opaque), or the skies as the
  gradient's stops computed on the device plus a transparent layer of the moon, wisps and
  hills, whichever is smaller in flash at an acceptable CPU cost (measure both; record).
- A drift test fails when `app/scene_rmr_art.c` falls behind the PNGs
  (`SIDOU_SCENE_UPDATE=1` regenerates it); a round-trip test decodes every sprite and
  background back to its PNG pixel for pixel; a test prints the flash it takes and fails
  over the 48 KB ceiling.

**Commit:** `feat(scene): RED MOON RITE's art converted into run-length sprites in flash`

### Task 3: A pixel layer for the grid: tiles composited on demand

**Files:** `app/grid.h`/`.c` (a painter region), `app/scene_px.h`/`.c` (new: the
compositor), tests (`tests/app/scene_px.rs`), `docs/art/README.md` ("Pixel scenes").

**Spec:**
- **No frame buffer.** The grid's pixel region today takes its pixels from a bitmap in
  flash. Add a second kind: a **painter** (a function and a context) that fills the pixels
  of the cells of a run as `grid_render_run` sends them. The grid still sends only stale
  cells; the scene marks the cells it changed (`grid_mark_stale(col, row)` or similar).
- **The compositor** (`scene_px`) draws a tile from layers in order: a background band (a
  bitmap, or a computed gradient), colour cycling over a mask (the torches), sprites in
  z-order (each with its position, mirror flag and an optional colour remap for the hit
  flash), procedural marks (the parry's spark rays, the spin's arc, small discs), and the
  HUD (font cells on rows 0-1). Each layer knows its bounding box, so a tile only visits the
  layers over it.
- **Which tiles changed:** every frame the scene lists candidate tiles (the union of each
  moving thing's last and new boxes, the cycling mask, the sky rows being wiped, the HUD's
  changed cells), composites each candidate into an 8 x 8 scratch, hashes it, and marks it
  stale only if the hash differs from the tile's last (a 32-bit hash a tile: 3,600 bytes).
  The renderer composites the stale ones again as it sends them (the cost of compositing a
  tile twice, measured, against the 28.8 KB a frame buffer would take).
- **Slicing:** the candidate pass runs half way between frames (`views_prepare`) in slices
  that keep each main-loop pass within `POLL_INSTRUCTIONS`; the send stays within the
  200-cell budget, taking the oldest stale tiles first so nothing starves.

**Tests:** host: a composited tile equals the reference (the same layers drawn by a Rust
model, and frames rendered by Task 1's script for a scripted event sequence, checked in
as goldens under `tests/golden/scene/`, regenerated with `SIDOU_SCENE_UPDATE=1` when Node is
present); mirrored sprites, transparency, z-order, remaps; the hash catches every change
in a random-layer fuzz and marks nothing on an unchanged frame; the existing grid tests
(regions, overlays, holds) still pass.

**Commit:** `feat(grid): a painter region and a tile compositor for pixel scenes`

### Task 4: RED MOON RITE on the device, driven by the music

**Files:** `app/scene_rmr.h`/`.c` (new: the vignette's state and logic), `app/scene_song.h`/
`.c` (new: the music hooks), `app/scene_view.c` (a sixth piece), `app/scene.h`
(`SCENE_PIECES`), `core/song/song_ext.c` (the `SCNE` chunk's voice roles), tests, goldens.

**The music hooks** (`scene_song`): from M2c's event ring (rows, note-ons with instrument,
gates) and chip A's registers (the cutoff), or from the instrument when no song plays,
build each frame's musical events:
- **roles of the voices:** DRUMS, BASS, ARP (or LEAD), from the song's `SCNE` chunk, with
  defaults by heuristics when it has none (a voice whose instruments start with noise is
  DRUMS; of the others the lowest-pitched over the last bar is BASS);
- **drum hits:** KICK, SNARE, HAT, from the `SCNE` chunk's instrument-to-drum table, else by
  pitch on the DRUMS voice (low a kick, middle a snare, high a hat; thresholds in the
  header);
- **position:** beat and bar from the row events and the rows a beat (the `MIDI` chunk, 4
  by default; 4 beats a bar); **the fill** is the last beat of every fourth bar;
- the bass's note, the arp's note-ons, the cutoff (0..2047), every voice's gate (a muted
  voice's part stops, as in M1's pieces).

**The vignette** (the prototype's `step` and `stages`, in C, with the disarm in place of
the helmet):
- the kick: the leader raises the sword and chops overhead; the snare: the other parries,
  a spark where the blades meet, and the leader loses a stamina notch; the hats: both shift
  between their two guards; the bass: a high bass gives the right barbarian the lead, a low
  one the left, and the pair drifts towards the one giving ground;
- **the fill:** the leader's spinning attack lands on the next downbeat and **disarms**
  barbarian 1: his sword flies up in an arc, spinning (the eight `sword-n` frames, a frame
  every two video frames), and **sticks point-down in the flagstones** (`sword-stuck`)
  with a small screen shake; he stands unarmed (the unarmed poses) until **bar 1**, when he
  takes the `reach` pose, pulls the sword from the floor and returns to guard. No helmet
  comes off, nothing is struck from the head, no blood;
- the arp: the torches flare; the cutoff: the sky's palette (three, from bright to
  blood-dark), changed by a band wiping down one cell row a frame; each bar the loser of
  the exchange loses stamina, an empty bar refills it; the HUD: as Task 1's review decided
  (eight pips a side or the blade meters) and `VS`.
- **Parallax (optional, if it fits):** the wisps over the moon drift a pixel every few
  frames, a band of their own, staggered so it never moves on a frame the sky wipes or the
  fill lands. Measure with and without; keep it only within the budget.
- Seeded and deterministic (as M1's pieces): the same music gives the same frames.

**Drawing it with Task 3's compositor** (`app/scene_px.h`; its device costs in
`docs/art/README.md` "Pixel scenes", from `emu-gate`'s `scene_budget.rs`). The prototype's
busiest frame, 176 tiles changed, is **feasible**: a candidate scanned costs about 2.8k
instructions and a cell sent about 2.9k, so a frame of some 250 candidates and 176 cells
sent comes to about 1.2M of the 5.2M a 40 ms frame leaves after the audio. It is not
feasible in one call, so the view must slice it across `ui_poll`s:
- **The scan runs in slices across the frame's `ui_poll`s, not in one `views_prepare`.**
  Each slice is `scene_px_scan(sp, g, SCENE_PX_SCAN_SLICE)` (16 candidates, at most about
  100k with the worst tiles the gate allows). A `ui_poll` call either scans one slice or
  renders runs (up to two of at most `SCENE_PX_RUN_CELLS` = 8 cells: about 71k measured,
  102k allowed), never both in one call. With `ui_poll` at 2 kHz, a 40 ms frame has about 80
  calls: 250 candidates take 16 slices, and 176 cells at least 22 runs, at least 11 calls.
  `ui.c` gets a hook for the slices (e.g. `views_idle`, called when there is nothing to send
  and no frame or prepare is due, returning whether work is left), and the gate measures
  the busiest call of each kind with the view running.
- **Candidates stay few:** set slots only when their layer changes. The sky's palette change
  wipes down a cell row a frame: two gradient and overlay slots (the old palette below the
  wipe, the new above it, their clips moving a row a frame), so a frame makes 30 candidates of
  it, not 480.
- **The refresh sweep:** `scene_px_refresh(sp, g, 1)` once a frame (2 if the budget allows).
- **The invariant** (`scene_px.h`): every rendered run reaches the panel, or the grid is
  invalidated. When SCENE has the screen back without going through `grid_region_painter`
  (which sends every cell), call `scene_px_init` (and set the slots again) or
  `grid_invalidate` before its next frame.
- **The bounds hold for RED MOON RITE's own frames:** the gate re-measures the worst tile and
  cell with the view (the fighters overlapping in a clash, the sword, the spark, the arc). If
  a cell costs more than `scene_budget.rs`'s `CELL_WORST`, lower `SCENE_PX_RUN_CELLS` or
  `SCENE_PX_SCAN_SLICE`; the bounds only tighten.

**Tests:**
- host: the hooks (roles, drum classification, the fill's timing, mutes) from scripted
  event streams; the vignette's state machine against Task 1's script for the same events
  (positions, poses, stamina, the sword's path and where it sticks);
- fm1-emu: SELECT reaches RED MOON RITE (its toast); goldens a second into the demo song,
  at the fill's landing and with the sword stuck; **the budget** over 60 s of the demo
  song: tiles a frame (average, most; against the prototype's 117 and 176), SPI a frame
  (average, worst), main loop a frame and the busiest `ui_poll` and pass, all within the
  bounds above, recorded in `docs/art/README.md`'s SCENE table; a muted drum voice stops
  the chops and parries; silence calms it (nothing sent after the last release);
- the audio stays bit-exact with the host while SCENE shows RED MOON RITE.

## Risks

- **Likeness:** the review and the user's sign-off at the end of Task 1 are the guard; the
  HUD has its own check and a ready replacement.
- **Flash:** 24 poses of 64 x 124 multicolour pixels are the bulk, plus the sword's nine
  small frames; the unarmed patches, shared rows and the computed sky are the levers, then
  fewer poses (spinB from spinA mirrored), then a smaller figure. The 48 KB ceiling holds
  the line.
- **CPU:** compositing twice per changed tile; if a frame's candidates are many (the sky's
  wipe plus the fill), the slices spread them and the cap delays a few tiles a frame.
- **The panel:** dithered ramps can shimmer on the real LCD; a hardware look is in the
  checklist.
- **Roles and drums in arbitrary songs** are guesses without the `SCNE` chunk; songs made in
  STEP can fill it (a later convenience), and the defaults are documented.
