# M2e: MIDI for songs, implementation plan

<!-- REUSE-IgnoreStart -->

> **For Claude:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: use superpowers:subagent-driven-development
> to implement this plan task by task.

**Goal:** design doc section 16's M2 row: clock in and out, start/stop/continue and song
position, the multitimbral and whole-chip modes (and omni), program change with bank
select for presets, instruments, songs and profiles, and SysEx: device inquiry and
instrument and song dumps, streamed, never buffered whole. So SIDOU sits in a MIDI setup as
a sequencer that follows or leads, and a DAW can play each SID voice as its own instrument.

**Overview and budgets:** [2026-10-15-m2-overview.md](2026-10-15-m2-overview.md).
Dependencies: Task 1 none; Tasks 2-4 need M2c Task 6 (the player on the device and its
command ring); Task 3 needs M2b Task 4 (`chip_ctl`, `keys_route`: which chip the keys
play); Task 4 needs M2c Task 8 (songs on the store); **Task 5 needs M2a Tasks 3 and 4**
(files on the store, claimed up front; the device's windows and quiet rule) **and M2c Tasks
2 and 8** (the `.sng` reader and writer; songs loaded and saved on the device, the save
freeze).

**Background to read first:**
- Design doc section 16 (all of it); decision record 0005 (timing; "Revisit sample-accurate
  placement: with M2's clock and transport"); `docs/reference/midi.md` (generated from
  `app/midi_map.c`; its drift test) and `docs/reference/controls.md` ("MIDI", "MIDI learn").
- `app/midi_in.h`/`.c` (the parser: real-time bytes reported anywhere as `MIDI_REALTIME`;
  **system common messages' data skipped today**, so song position `F2` and song select `F3`
  need decoding), `app/midi_rx.h`, `app/midi_ring.h`, `app/midi_usb.h`, `app/midi_out.h`
  (packets, rate limits, no echo), `app/midi_map.h`, `app/midi_learn.h`,
  `platform/fm1/midi.c` (the TRS and USB paths, the stamps in 24 MHz ticks, the update
  protocol's SysEx: frames `F0 22 24 35 ...` and `00 59` handled by `ota.c` through `usb.c`).
- M2c: `core/player/song_ctl.h` (play, stop, continue, the state hand-over for seeks, jam
  notes, the frame period), decision record 0007, `docs/reference/song-format.md` (the
  `MIDI` chunk).
- `docs/TODO.md` ("fm1-emu does not emulate USB": MIDI in the emulator is TRS only; USB is
  host-tested and checked on hardware).

**Rules:** as in [M2a](2026-10-15-m2a-storage.md) ("Rules"). Plus: SIDOU's SysEx uses the
non-commercial manufacturer ID `0x7D` (design section 16) and must never collide with the
update protocol's frames; **never send `F0 22 24 35 7D F7`**.

**Budgets:** every main-loop pass within `POLL_INSTRUCTIONS` with a clock, a flood of CCs
and a SysEx stream arriving together; the latency gate (`latency.rs`) unchanged for live
notes; the audio interrupt unchanged except the command ring's pops (M2c); RAM ≤ 2 KB for
the clock follower and the SysEx stream state; **free RAM (RAM region plus POOL) ≥ 13,500
bytes** after this plan; image ≤ 452,000 bytes after this plan.

### Task 1: Real-time and system common messages; the clock follower (portable)

**Files:** `app/midi_in.c`/`.h` (decode `F2` song position, 14 bits, and `F3` song select;
keep every other behaviour), `app/midi_clock.h`/`.c` (new), tests
(`tests/app/midi_clock.rs`, `tests/app/midi.rs`).

**Spec:**
- `midi_in` reports `MIDI_SONG_POSITION` (the 14-bit value) and `MIDI_SONG_SELECT`; a real
  time byte inside them is still reported where it occurs.
- **The clock follower** takes timing clocks (`F8`) with their arrival stamps, in samples
  (the stamps M1a records, converted as decision record 0005 does) and gives:
  - the beat period, smoothed (a second-order loop or a least-squares fit over the last
    24-48 clocks; choose and justify in the header), locking within two beats of a clock
    starting, holding to within 0.1 % of a steady tempo and its phase within 1 ms despite
    the input's jitter (the TRS poll's 0.5 ms; USB's 1 ms frames), following a tempo ramp
    without overshooting by more than 1 %, and declaring the clock lost after 300 ms
    without one;
  - for the player: **the frame period in samples** (Q16) that makes the song's rows land on
    the clock: with GoatTracker's tempo T frames a row and R rows a beat (the song's `MIDI`
    chunk, 4 by default), a beat is T × R frames, so the frame period is the beat period
    divided by T × R (funktempo: the pair's average, so each pair of rows spans its share);
    and a small phase correction at each beat so drift never accumulates;
  - `FA` start, `FB` continue, `FC` stop and `F2` positions as transport events;
  - **holds:** the store's held erases (M2a Task 4) mask interrupts for up to 35 ms with
    the transport stopped, and the TRS bytes that arrive meanwhile are stamped late, in a
    bunch. The follower is told of each hold (its start and end stamps) and drops the
    clocks stamped inside it or within one clock period after, rather than reading them as
    jitter; the steady-tempo and phase bounds hold across a hold.
- Pure state machine, integer or float without libm, no allocation.

**Tests:** synthetic clocks at 60, 120, 125.3 and 200 BPM with jitter like the TRS poll's
and like USB's, tempo steps and ramps, dropped clocks, a clock that stops; the lock time,
steady error, phase error and ramp overshoot asserted at the bounds above; a 35 ms hold in
a steady 120 BPM clock leaves the tempo within 0.1 % and the phase within 1 ms; the frame period
for tempo 6 at 125.3 BPM equals the free-running PAL frame within 0.1 %; SPP and song
select decoded, with real-time bytes inside them.

**Commit:** `feat(midi): song position and song select; a clock follower for the player`

### Task 2: Clock and transport on the device, in and out

**Files:** `platform/fm1/midi.c`, `app/midi_route.c` (new here, extended in Task 3),
`core/player/song_ctl.c` (the frame period command, used by M2c's frame clock),
`app/midi_out.c` (real-time out), `app/views.c` and `app/settings.c` (the CLOCK settings),
tests.

**Spec:**
- **Clock in** from TRS and USB, merged as all MIDI is: with CLOCK set to FOLLOW (or AUTO,
  the default: follow while a clock arrives), the player's frame period follows the clock
  follower; without a clock the song runs on its own frames.
- **Transport in:** start plays the song from its start on the next clock (the next frame
  at the clock's phase); continue resumes; stop stops (releases ring, M2c); a song position
  seeks: the main loop fast-forwards a shadow player to the position (SPP is in 16ths:
  rows = SPP × R / 4) in slices within the poll allowance and hands the state over
  (`song_ctl`'s state hand-over); a continue that arrives before the seek finishes starts
  when it finishes, jumping ahead by the frames missed. `F3` selects the subtune.
- **Clock out** over USB (the FM-1 has no TRS out) while SIDOU runs on its own frames:
  24 clocks a beat from the player's frame count (T × R frames a beat), timed from the
  event ring's frame numbers and sent at the USB poll, plus start, stop, continue and SPP
  when the panel's transport changes. Off by default (`CLOCK OUT` setting), persisted.
  Never echo a clock that came in (SIDOU following a clock does not send one).
- **Sequenced timing, decision record 0005 revisited:** the player's frames are already
  sample-exact (M2c Task 6). Notes from MIDI that drive the player (Task 3's jam notes) may
  carry their stamp through `song_ctl` and be applied at their sample with a fixed delay of
  one block plus a poll (the "TIGHT" timing 0005 sketched), as an option; live notes to chip
  B stay as M1 decided. Measure the cost, decide, and record the result as an amendment to
  0005.

**Tests:**
- fm1-emu over TRS: a 120 BPM clock with poll-like jitter makes the demo song's rows land
  on the clock (the row events' sample times against the clocks': the 16th-note phase
  error within 1 ms after two beats); start, stop, continue, SPP to bar 3 then continue
  (the position the events report); a clock that stops: the song carries on at the last
  tempo or stops (decide and document); the main loop within the allowance during a seek;
  the latency gate unchanged.
- host: clock out's packets (24 a beat at the song's tempo, from a synthetic event stream;
  start/stop/SPP when the transport changes; nothing while following).

**Docs:** `docs/reference/midi.md` (generated: a "Clock and transport" section),
`docs/reference/controls.md` (CLOCK, CLOCK OUT), decision record 0005 (amendment),
`docs/hardware-test.md` (follow a DAW's clock over USB and TRS; lead a DAW).

**Commit:** `feat(midi): follow and send MIDI clock; start, stop, continue and song position`

### Task 3: Modes: live, multitimbral, whole-chip, omni

**Files:** `app/midi_route.h`/`.c`, `app/live.c`, `app/chip_ctl.c`,
`core/player/song_ctl.c` (jam notes, their pitch bend), `core/song/song_ext.c` (the
`MIDI` chunk), `app/views.c` (a MIDI page in the menu), tests.

**Spec** (design section 16, "Modes"; saved per song in the `MIDI` chunk, with a global
default in the settings):
- **LIVE** (M1's): a channel (or omni) plays the instrument, on the chip the keys play
  (M2b Task 4: chip A with no song playing, chip B while one plays, A with KEYS A),
  three-voice polyphony with stealing, or mono/legato.
- **MULTITIMBRAL:** channels N, N+1, N+2 play chip A's voices 1, 2, 3, each as a jam voice
  of the player with its own GoatTracker instrument (Task 4's program change picks it), its
  own pitch bend (range per channel, RPN 0) and its own notes (mono per channel, last note
  priority, legato option); channel N+3 plays chip B (the instrument). A jam note on a voice
  takes it from the song while it sounds (the song's writes to that voice are held back,
  as M2c's mute does) and gives it back at its release. Chip-level CCs on chip A's
  channels act on chip A (character and profile through its `chip_ctl`); filter CCs (74,
  71) on them set an override of the song's filter writes, like a lock that holds until
  CC 121 or the mode changes.
- **WHOLE-CHIP:** one channel plays all three voices of a chip: on chip A with one GT2
  instrument (three-voice polyphony, stealing), on chip B as the instrument. Options:
  CHORD (one key plays a stored chord over the three voices) and ARP (the classic
  one-voice chord arpeggio: held notes cycled on one voice at a rate set in frames, or
  synced to the clock, so the other two voices stay free).
- **Chip A belongs to the player in MULTITIMBRAL and in WHOLE-CHIP on chip A,** song or
  not: `keys_route` treats these modes as "a song playing", so the instrument (the panel's
  keys, and channel N+3 in MULTITIMBRAL) plays chip B, with the keys' setup (M2b Task 4).
- **OMNI** for quick setups: every channel to LIVE.
- **The MIDI page** (menu): MODE, the base channel N, the rows a beat for the clock, CLOCK
  and CLOCK OUT (Task 2); within the views' budgets.
- MIDI learn and the CC map keep working in every mode (learned CCs on the LIVE channel).

**Tests:** host: the router for each mode and channel; voice ownership between the song and
jam notes (a jam note's voice silent of the song while it sounds, the song's writes back
after); pitch bend per channel; CHORD and ARP; the `MIDI` chunk round trip; fm1-emu over
TRS: in MULTITIMBRAL, notes on N, N+1, N+2 sound on chip A's voices with their
instruments (audio against the host's render of the same jam), N+3 on chip B; WHOLE-CHIP's
polyphony; every pass within the allowance with a dense four-channel stream.

**Docs:** `docs/reference/midi.md` (generated: the modes), `docs/reference/controls.md`
(the MIDI page), `CHANGELOG.md`.

**Commit:** `feat(midi): live, multitimbral, whole-chip and omni modes, saved with the song`

**Results (Task 3, built before M2c Task 6 reached `main`):**
- `app/midi_route.h`/`.c`: the modes as a portable router between `midi_rx` and the
  player, host-tested (`tests/app/midi_route.rs`); the MIDI page (`app/views.c`, HOME, MIDI:
  MODE, CHANNEL, OPTION, LEGATO, CHORD 2/3, ARP RATE, ROWS/BEAT; CLOCK and CLOCK OUT come with
  Task 2); the `MIDI` chunk's options appended after its channels (`core/song/song_ext.c`,
  `docs/reference/song-format.md`); `keys_route_set_player`, which counts as a song playing.
  Deviations: OMNI is a mode of its own and the default (M1's behaviour unchanged); WHOLE-CHIP
  A's and MULTITIMBRAL's instrument channel is N + 3 in both; WHOLE-CHIP B also gives chip A
  to the player (that is what sends the instrument to chip B); program change n on a chip A
  channel is GoatTracker instrument n + 1 (0..62). The setting is in RAM until M2a's
  settings persist it.
- **What `song_ctl` must add to bind the router's player interface** (`midi_route_player`,
  its contract in `app/midi_route.h`; all additive, nothing of M2c Task 6 changes): (1) **jam
  take-over**: a jam note takes its voice from the song, the song's writes to it held back
  as the mute does but without the gate-off, while the player's channel plays the jam note
  (`song_ctl_jam` today plays it as the next row and the song's next row takes it back); (2)
  **jam release**: `jam_off(voice)`, the jam note's gate off and the voice given back to the
  song at its next row, its registers restored from the shadow; (3) **legato**: `jam_on`'s
  legato flag changes the note without a new attack; (4) **jam bend**: a per-voice pitch
  offset (`LIVE_PITCH_SEMITONE` per semitone) on the jam notes' frequency, kept across notes;
  (5) **filter lock**: chip A's cutoff and resonance held at the router's values over the
  song's writes ($15-$17 less the routing), given back with locks 0; (6) the jam working
  with no song loaded (an empty song), since MULTITIMBRAL owns chip A song or not. The
  device's `midi_init` then passes the bound interface to `midi_route_init`
  (`platform/fm1/midi.c`); each call returns 0 when the command ring is full (the router
  drops a note and resends a bend or a filter).
- fm1-emu (`midi_modes.rs`): MULTITIMBRAL from the MIDI page moves the instrument to chip B,
  N + 3 plays it there, chip A's channels reach the router's jam voices and play nothing on
  the instrument; OMNI gives chip A back. The audio of jam notes against a host render waits
  for the binding above. A dense four-channel stream (notes, bends, CCs, 2,382 messages in
  2 s at TRS's full rate): the busiest pass 24.2k instructions (allowance 120k).
- Costs: the image 363,644 to 371,464 bytes (+7,820); RAM region `.bss` +208 bytes, POOL
  and RAMTEXT unchanged; free RAM (RAM region plus POOL) 97,008 bytes; the audio interrupt
  untouched; a USB burst of 16 of the instrument's CCs drains in 7,892 instructions (7,751
  before: the router's per-channel table costs about 9 a message). The MIDI page's busiest `ui_poll` 20.6k (bound 23k), its busiest frame 10.3 ms
  of SPI (the wipe), idle 2 windows in 16 frames.

### Task 4: Program change and bank select

**Files:** `app/midi_route.c`, `app/live.c`, `app/song_io.c` (M2c), `app/chip_ctl.c`,
`app/midi_out.c`, `app/midi_map.c` (CC 0 and 32 as bank select), tests.

**Spec:**
- **Bank select** (CC 0 MSB, CC 32 LSB, per channel, held until changed) chooses what a
  program change on that channel means: bank 0 the channel's sound (the instrument's
  presets on a LIVE or chip B channel, M1's meaning; a GoatTracker instrument 1-63 on a
  chip A channel in MULTITIMBRAL or WHOLE-CHIP), bank 1 **songs** (program n loads the
  store's n-th song in name order, stopped, on the LIVE or base channel only), bank 2 the
  chip's **profiles** (0-4 the built-in ones, 5-12 USER 1-8).
- A song loaded by a program change takes several passes (M2c Task 8); a second one
  arriving meanwhile replaces the request.
- **Out:** the panel's preset change sends its program change as M1d does; a song loaded
  from the SONG menu sends bank 1 and its program.
- Update `docs/reference/midi.md`'s generator so the chart lists the banks.

**Tests:** host: bank state per channel; each bank's action; the out messages; fm1-emu over
TRS: bank 1 program 0 loads the first stored song (the event ring shows it playing after a
start), bank 2 program 5 picks USER 1.

**Commit:** `feat(midi): program change with bank select for sounds, songs and profiles`

### Task 5: SysEx: inquiry, instrument and song dumps, the store's directory

**Files:** `app/sysex.h`/`.c` (new, portable: SIDOU's SysEx, a streaming state machine),
`platform/fm1/midi.c` (routing SIDOU's frames to it, replies over USB),
`host/crates/sidou-sysex/` (new: a library that builds and parses SIDOU's messages, used by
the tests and later by the companion and the web installer, M4), `docs/reference/sysex.md`
(new), tests.

**Spec** (`docs/reference/sysex.md` is the specification; write it first):
- **Universal device inquiry** (`F0 7E <id> 06 01 F7`) answered with an identity reply
  (`F0 7E 7F 06 02 7D <family> <member> <version> F7`; SIDOU's version in the four bytes).
  The update protocol's own identity request (`ota.c`, cmd `0x11`) keeps working unchanged.
- **SIDOU's frames:** `F0 7D 53 44 <cmd> <seq> <data, 7-bit packed: 8 bytes for every 7>
  <checksum> F7` ("SD"); none of them can be mistaken for the update protocol's or the stock
  soft key's. Commands:
  - `INQ` (SIDOU's own: firmware, store format, free space);
  - `DIR` (the store's files: name, type, length, CRC-32), streamed in packets;
  - `GET` a file, an instrument of the current song (GoatTracker's `.ins`, `GTI5`: readme
    section 6.2) or the current song (its `.sng` with the trailer, written on the fly by
    M2c's writer, never assembled in RAM);
  - `PUT` a file (to the store, streamed through M2a's file writer: a song, or any file
    type), an instrument (`GTI5`, its tables relocated into the current song's free table
    rows, as GoatTracker does on load), or "load this song now";
  - `DEL` a file;
  - `ACK`/`NAK` per packet (sequence number, checksum), so a host paces itself on USB; a
    `PUT` over TRS (no reply path) runs without acknowledgements, paced by the sender, and
    is checked by the file's CRC-32 at its end.
- Packets carry at most 256 data bytes; SysEx bytes go to the state machine as they arrive
  (`midi_in`'s SysEx callback), so a dump is never held whole. Abort, timeout (2 s without
  a byte), a bad checksum and a full store all end the transfer cleanly with a reply (on
  USB) and a toast; a half-written file never commits (M2a Task 3).
- **The store's idle timeout:** the store aborts an open file that takes no byte for 5 s
  (`store_file_idle`, M2a; the store's own backstop, longer than this protocol's 2 s). A
  `PUT` that sends its `ACK` only once a packet is programmed must count the flash's program
  time in each gap (packets under 256 bytes at the slowest write window, M2a Task 3b's
  numbers, can take several passes), so the 2 s timeout here counts from the last `ACK`
  sent, never from the last byte received, and the store's 5 s is never reached first.
- **A `PUT` claims its space before its first `ACK`.** The first packet of a `PUT` carries
  the file's length; the state machine calls `store_file_begin` (M2a Task 3), which claims
  every sector the file needs from the erase-ahead pool before it returns. Only then is
  the first `ACK` sent, so no byte of a transfer ever waits for an erase. If the pool
  cannot cover the file now, the reply is `NAK BUSY` with the pool's state, and the host
  retries after a pause (the pool refills when the device is quiet, M2a Task 4); over TRS,
  with no reply path, the transfer is refused with a toast (`STORE BUSY: RESEND`) and its
  bytes are dropped. A file larger than the store can ever hold is `NAK FULL`.
- The quiet rule (M2a Task 4) treats a running transfer as not quiet, so no refill erase
  starts in the middle of a stream; what makes the stream safe is the claim above, not
  the quiet rule: the pool (≥ `STORE_POOL_MIN`, 20 sectors) holds the largest file plus
  GC's reserve. A `PUT` of the current song while the device saves it waits for the save's
  end (M2c Task 8's freeze), replying `NAK BUSY`.

**Tests:**
- host (`sidou-sysex` and `tests/app/sysex.rs`): every command round trips between the
  library and the C state machine; 7-bit packing at every length; checksums; aborts,
  timeouts, a full store; a `GTI5` instrument relocated into a song with its tables;
  **fuzz** the C state machine with random and mutated streams (ASan, UBSan); the update
  protocol's frames are not taken by SIDOU's machine and vice versa;
- fm1-emu over TRS: `PUT` a song file, then the store holds it byte for byte and it loads
  and plays; `PUT` an instrument; a 64 KiB `PUT` at TRS's full rate with a chord held: no
  erase and no hold from the first packet to the end (the flash log shows programs only);
  a `PUT` with the pool drained (a test hook) is refused before any byte is written; every pass within the poll allowance during a stream at
  TRS's full rate; the audio budget unaffected.
- Replies (USB only) are checked on the host through the USB packetiser, as M1d's MIDI out
  is.

## Risks

- **USB is untested in fm1-emu:** clock out, SysEx replies and USB input are host-tested
  and checked on hardware.
- **Song position on long songs** costs a fast-forward of the player (about 5k
  instructions a frame); a jump to minute 5 is 15,000 frames, about 75 M instructions,
  spread over passes: about 0.3 s at 240 MHz. Measure; if too slow, keep periodic
  snapshots of the player's state while playing.
- **Jam notes against the song** on the same voice: the take-over rule is simple and may
  surprise; to try with a DAW.
- **The SysEx protocol** becomes a public interface the companion (M4) depends on: version
  it from the first frame (`INQ` reports it).
