M2 turns SIDOU from a live instrument into a song machine: songs in GoatTracker 2's own
`.sng` format, played by a C player on the device and checked write for write against
GoatTracker's 6502 player; the STEP and TRACKER views to make them on the FM-1; flash
storage; chip B; MIDI clock, transport, modes and dumps; and the SCENE view's first
music-driven vignette, RED MOON RITE.

The work is split into six plans, each a coherent sub-milestone executed task by task by
fresh implementer subagents (superpowers:subagent-driven-development) and reviewed, as M1's
were. Every task in them stands alone: it names its files, its spec, its tests, its gates,
the docs it updates and its commit message. Two tasks stop for the user: M2a Task 3b (a
flash timing spike run on a real FM-1) and the end of M2f Task 1 (the originality sign-off).

## The design changes, and why

**"Native format" becomes "GT2 `.sng` as native".** Design doc section 14's M2 list said
"native format and player" and "GT2 import, with the register-match tests", and section 5
described a chunked `SIDO` format "close to GoatTracker 2". The user decided (2026-10-15)
that SIDOU reads and writes GoatTracker 2's `.sng` directly instead:

- GT2 is the most widely used editable C64 song format; its files open in GoatTracker on
  every desktop, so a song made on the FM-1 can be finished in GoatTracker and back.
- A second format "close to GT2" would need its own importer, exporter, migrations and
  fixtures for no musical gain.
- What round-trips is stated exactly (M2c Task 1): a **canonical `GTS5` file** (one that
  GoatTracker's own load and save leave unchanged) round-trips byte for byte; older idents
  are converted on load and saved as `GTS5`; name-only instruments are dropped on save, as
  GoatTracker's `savesong` drops them.
- SIDOU's extras (per-step parameter locks for character and filter, chip setups, the
  effect slots, the player options GoatTracker keeps outside the file) go in a **trailer
  that GoatTracker ignores**: GoatTracker's loader reads the `.sng` sections in order and
  stops. The trailer's start is shaped so GoatTracker Stereo's trial read of six orderlists
  cannot mistake a mono file with a trailer for a stereo one (M2c Task 1). Chunked and
  versioned as section 5 asked, so the old "readers skip chunks they don't recognise" rule
  survives inside the trailer.

The design doc is amended where M2 changes it (sections 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11, 14 and 16),
each with an "Amended (M2 plan, 2026-10-15)" note:
- section 5: the song format and the player's place (the audio interrupt); the live view's
  chip (the keys);
- section 6: the GT2 row ("is the format", not "parse into the native format"); SID-Wizard
  stays in M5, where section 14 already put it;
- section 7: SCENE's first device piece is drawn pixel art (RED MOON RITE), the TRACKER's
  moving bar, CHIP's effect slots shared by both chips;
- section 8: the 6502's share is about 195-200 instructions a sample, not the ≤ 400 the
  table budgeted (below);
- section 9: the register-match test uses a host-only 6502;
- section 11: GoatTracker 2 and the `mos6502` crate in the licensing table;
- section 14: M2's list;
- section 16: the live channel plays the keys' chip.

## Decisions that span the plans

1. **GT2 `.sng` is the song file.** GTS5 written, GTS2-GTS5 read (as GoatTracker reads
   them). SIDOU's extension is a trailer after the last pattern: `SIDOU-X` magic, a version,
   a lead-in, tagged chunks, a CRC-32 of the GT2 part it belongs to and of itself (M2c Task
   1). The device plays multispeed 1-4.
2. **The player is C, and it runs in the audio interrupt,** at sample-exact frame
   boundaries (the block is split at the boundary, as decision record 0005 described for
   notes). The song and its edits reach it through a command ring; its row and note events
   come back through an event ring. Why the interrupt and not the main loop: a frame is then
   exactly 19,656 C64 cycles, whatever the main loop does (a chip preparation, a flash
   write, the USB retry); the host renders a song bit for bit as the device does, so the
   emulator gate's host-against-target check covers songs; and it is the same model M3's
   6502 will use. The cost: its tick joins the audio budget (gated, M2c Task 6) and its code
   joins RAMTEXT (projected below). The 6502 and the song player never run together (chip
   A plays a song or a `.sid`), so they share the 6502's headroom.
3. **Song edits reach the interrupt as O(1) steps only:** a cell write (a row's 4 bytes, an
   instrument or table byte, an orderlist entry) or a swap of a pattern's or orderlist's
   index entry to a copy the main loop has already rebuilt in free arena space. At most 8 a
   block, inside the player's gate (M2c Tasks 2 and 6, M2d Task 3).
4. **The reference is GoatTracker's own packed player** (`player.s`, relocated by
   GoatTracker's `gt2reloc` with the song's options), run in a **host-only 6502**: the
   `mos6502` crate (0.10.1, BSD-3-Clause) behind a small trait, which traps any
   undocumented opcode executed, so M3's device 6502 (C, in `core/cpu6502/`) can later be
   cross-checked against it; M3's illegal opcodes are checked by Tom Harte's tests, not
   this harness. GoatTracker's source (GPL-2.0-or-later) is test-only and never linked into
   the firmware; SIDOU's player is written from the readme and `player.s`'s behaviour,
   credited, GPL-3.0-or-later (which "or later" permits).
5. **SID-Wizard import stays in M5,** as design section 14 already has it.
6. **Which chip the keys play (the user's decision, 2026-10-16):** with no song playing,
   the keys play **chip A, as in M1**; while a song plays on chip A, they play **chip B**; a
   **KEYS** setting (AUTO, the default, or A) keeps them on chip A during a song, taking a
   voice from the song while a key holds it. **The keys' setup travels with the keys:** the
   instrument owns one chip setup (M1's 6581 R4AR by default), chip B always holds it (it
   no longer defaults to the 8580 R5), so the keys sound the same on either chip; chip A
   takes the song's setup only while the song plays (M2b Task 4).
7. **SAVE (the user's decision, 2026-10-16):** a press of SAVE saves: the profile into a
   USER slot on the CHIP view (focus on the chip, a USER slot targeted or applied), the
   song in the song views (STEP, TRACKER and its pages), the settings elsewhere. **SAVE
   held while a knob turns opens MIDI learn** for that knob, the frame opening on the
   turn; in the frame, SAVE forgets, **SAVE held + HOME** clears all mappings after a
   confirm, HOME cancels (M2a Task 5's table). This rewrites M1d's learn gesture, its tests
   and docs.
8. **The store lives in [0x97000, 0xE0000)**, 292 KiB, the range the update loader never
   writes or erases and that the record scan skips (`ldr_core.c`), inside the package's VM
   descriptor (`[0x93000, 0xE9000)`, which nothing on SIDOU acts on); log-structured, CRC'd;
   an **erase-ahead pool** of at least 20 sectors (the largest file plus GC's reserve)
   claimed before a write's first byte; page programs in windows that start within 250 µs
   of an audio block's end and last at most 1,000 µs; erases only when quiet, held at most
   35 ms (M2a).
9. **The effect slots stay one chain, on the mix of both chips.** Design section 7 asked for
   slots per chip; a second chain's 128 KiB delay and reverb pool does not fit (see RAM
   below). The CHIP view's B tab shows chip B's setup, and the slots as shared.
10. **SCENE's vignette is drawn pixel art,** composited per tile on demand (no frame
   buffer), with only original art; the prototype's drawing code is in the repository
   (`assets/prototypes/sidou-vignettes.html`) and is recovered as the art's source (M2f
   Task 1). RED MOON RITE's fill is a disarm (the sword spins away and sticks in the
   flagstones). **DUSK DUEL is dropped from M2** (the user's decision: too close to its
   genre's best-known game).

**M3's 6502 needs the C64's 64 KB address space:** it overlays the song's content region
(a song and a `.sid` never play at once), so the region is one contiguous 64 KiB block with
a link-map symbol from the start. M3 then has the last ~12 KB, less what its RAMTEXT takes,
for the 6502's state, the PSID header and the browser. If that is short, the levers are: a
smaller slot pool (128 KiB of delay and reverb; 96 KiB caps the delay at about 0.74 s and
frees 32 KB), or a paged 6502 memory. **That choice is the user's, at M3's planning;** M2
must not spend the margin.

**Audio interrupt.** Design section 8 budgeted **≤ 400 instructions a sample** for the
6502; the gates M1 left give it **205** (docs/TODO.md, "The 6502 must fit the audio
headroom the gates leave", which keeps the levers). That shortfall predates M2. M2's
always-on additions make it slightly worse: chip B's second render call, its fade and the
mix (~3 a sample, measured by M2b Task 3), the command ring's pop (< 1). The quiet
detector's peak scan (~2) runs only while an erase is queued with the transport stopped,
so never under a song or a `.sid`. **M3 keeps about 195-200** for the 6502. In song mode:
the player's tick and the block's edits, gated at **≤ 60 a sample in their worst block**
(a tick of ~6k instructions in one 128-sample block; M2c Task 6); **multispeed is capped
at 4 on the device**, because at 8 or more two frames can fall in one block (about 94 a
sample); and the frame split (a few a sample). Song mode and M3's 6502 never run together.

**Main loop:** every pass within `POLL_INSTRUCTIONS` (120k), and M2b Task 1 retires the one
exception (the chip reset) by preparing the spare chip a slice (≤ 40k) a tick. The store's
work is ≤ 30k a pass. Saving, loading, parsing a song, SysEx and the SCENE compositor all
work in slices that keep each pass inside the allowance; each plan's emu tests assert it.

**SPI:** every view holds to 200 cells a frame (17.5 ms with windows) as LIVE, SCENE and
CHIP do; RED MOON RITE's prototype measured 117 tiles a frame on average and 176 at most.

## Hardware flags (M2 items only a real FM-1 can settle)

- **The flash part's program and erase times** (`85 60 14`, a Puya P25Q80-class 8 Mbit
  part; fm1-emu makes both instantaneous), its 256-byte page erase (0x81) and
  erase-suspend: **measured by M2a Task 3b's spike before M2a Task 4**, whose decision rule
  picks the window size and the erase mode.
- Whether a 1,000 µs masked window ever drops an audio block or a TRS byte, and how a 35 ms
  held erase sounds on a release tail.
- **The SPL's view of the package's VM descriptor** (`[0x93000, 0xE9000)`): that stock V15
  and the SPL leave the store alone (Felucca stores user data there, and its loader skips
  it), checked by M2a Task 3b's stock round trip; and that nothing in the store can look
  like an update record to the SPL (the guard band, by construction, tested).
- **The CPU clock:** custom firmwares run at 360 MHz on these units, but every budget in M2
  assumes 240 MHz (docs/hardware.md). At 360 MHz every instruction-count margin grows by
  half; the spike records which clock the unit runs. The plans' gates stay at 240 MHz.
- **The button ids M2d depends on:** LFO 5, GLO 7, ARP 10, SEQ 11, PLAY 12, REC 13 are
  Felucca's and unconfirmed on a SIDOU unit (M1 used FX, SCL, ENV, EDIT, HOME and SAVE).
  M2d Task 1 adds the check to `docs/hardware-test.md`.
- Two SIDs and the player at 240 MHz against the instruction counts.
- MIDI clock out and SysEx replies over USB (fm1-emu has no USB).
- The LCD's look of RED MOON RITE's dither and colour ramps on the real panel.

## The user's decisions (2026-10-16), and what remains a risk

Decided, and written into the tasks: SAVE's gesture (decision 7; M2a Task 5), which chip
the keys play and the keys' setup (decision 6; M2b Task 4), DUSK DUEL dropped and RED MOON
RITE's disarm (decision 10; M2f), the originality sign-off at the end of M2f Task 1 with a
HUD check. Risks that stay:

- **Chip B song tracks** stay in M5 (design section 14). M2 stores chip B's *setup* in the
  extension and reserves the chunk for its tracks; STEP shows chip B's lane greyed.
- **Effect slots per chip** (design section 7) do not fit; M2 keeps one chain on the mix.
- **RAM for M3** (above): the content region and the levers; the user decides at M3.
- **GoatTracker compatibility of the trailer.** GoatTracker 2.77's loader ignores trailing
  bytes; GoatTracker Stereo's trial read is handled by the trailer's lead-in (M2c Task 1
  lists the tools checked). If a tool rewrites the file, the trailer is gone and the song
  plays as a plain GT2 song: documented, not prevented.
- **Equivalence is per frame, not per cycle.** The test compares each frame's ordered SID
  writes; the C player cannot reproduce the 6502's write timing inside a frame. The device
  applies a frame's writes at the frame's sample. Documented in M2c Task 1.
- **RED MOON RITE's likeness.** It is a homage to late-80s fantasy sword-fighting games;
  the best known of them is a C64 classic with a barbarian duel. The art must not copy its
  sprites, poses, arena, colours, HUD, logo or its decapitation; the fill is a disarm. M2f
  Task 1 ends with a side-by-side review the user signs off before any art is converted.
- **Design doc's tracker note.** Design section 7 did not say the tracker uses a moving bar
  by default; the prototype page did (and the user's brief). Section 7 is amended to say so.
