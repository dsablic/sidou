# M2: Songs, overview and index

<!-- REUSE-IgnoreStart -->

**Status:** plan, for review (written 2026-10-15/16 on branch `m2-plan`, from `main` at
1a862f5, where M1 is complete).

M2 turns SIDOU from a live instrument into a song machine: songs in GoatTracker 2's own
`.sng` format, played by a C player on the device and checked write for write against
GoatTracker's 6502 player; the STEP and TRACKER views to make them on the FM-1; flash
storage; chip B; MIDI clock, transport, modes and dumps; and the SCENE view's first
music-driven vignette, RED MOON RITE.

The work is split into six plans, each a coherent sub-milestone executed task by task by
fresh implementer subagents (superpowers:subagent-driven-development) and reviewed, as M1's
were. Every task in them stands alone: it names its files, its spec, its tests, its gates,
the docs it updates and its commit message.

A sensible serial order for one controller: **M2a, M2c 1-5, M2b, M2c 6-8, M2d, M2e, M2f**,
with M2f's art tasks and M2e Task 1 slotted in wherever a reviewer is waiting. Two
controllers can run M2a and M2c's host tasks side by side: they touch different files.

## The design changes, and why

**"Native format" becomes "GT2 `.sng` as native".** Design doc section 14's M2 list said
"native format and player" and "GT2 import, with the register-match tests", and section 5
described a chunked `SIDO` format "close to GoatTracker 2". The user decided (2026-10-15)
that SIDOU reads and writes GoatTracker 2's `.sng` directly instead:

- GT2 is the most widely used editable C64 song format; its files open in GoatTracker on
  every desktop, so a song made on the FM-1 can be finished in GoatTracker and back.
- A second format "close to GT2" would need its own importer, exporter, migrations and
  fixtures for no musical gain, and its "lossless" claim would always need proving. With
  the `.sng` itself as the file, the GT2 part is lossless by construction.
- SIDOU's extras (per-step parameter locks for character and filter, chip B's setup, the
  effect slots, the chip profile and character, the player options GoatTracker keeps
  outside the file) go in a **trailer that GoatTracker ignores**: GoatTracker's loader reads
  the `.sng` sections in order and stops (M2c Task 1 documents the evidence and the exact
  round-trip rules). Chunked and versioned as section 5 asked, so the old "readers skip
  chunks they don't recognise" rule survives inside the trailer.

The design doc is amended where M2 changes it (section 5, 6, 7, 9, 11 and 14), each with an
"Amended (M2 plan, 2026-10-15)" note:
- section 5: the song format and the player's place (the audio interrupt);
- section 6: the GT2 row ("is the format", not "parse into the native format"); SID-Wizard
  stays in M5, where section 14 already put it;
- section 7: SCENE's first device piece is drawn pixel art (RED MOON RITE), the TRACKER's
  moving bar, CHIP's effect slots shared by both chips;
- section 9: the register-match test uses a host-only 6502;
- section 11: GoatTracker 2 and the `mos6502` crate in the licensing table;
- section 14: M2's list.

## Decisions that span the plans

1. **GT2 `.sng` is the song file.** GTS5 written, GTS2-GTS5 read (as GoatTracker reads
   them). SIDOU's extension is a trailer after the last pattern: `SIDOU-X` magic, a version,
   tagged chunks, a CRC-32 of the GT2 part it belongs to and of itself (M2c Task 1).
2. **The player is C, and it runs in the audio interrupt,** at sample-exact frame
   boundaries (the block is split at the boundary, as decision record 0005 described for
   notes). The song and its edits reach it through a command ring; its row and note events
   come back through an event ring. Why the interrupt and not the main loop: a frame is then
   exactly 19,656 C64 cycles, whatever the main loop does (a chip reset, a flash write, the
   USB retry); the host renders a song bit for bit as the device does, so the emulator
   gate's host-against-target check covers songs; and it is the same model M3's 6502 will
   use. The cost: its tick joins the audio budget (gated, M2c Task 6) and its code joins
   RAMTEXT (projected below). The 6502 and the song player never run together (chip A plays
   a song or a `.sid`), so they share the 6502's headroom.
3. **The reference is GoatTracker's own packed player** (`player.s`, relocated by
   GoatTracker's `gt2reloc` with the song's options), run in a **host-only 6502**: the
   `mos6502` crate (0.10.1, BSD-3-Clause) behind a small trait, so M3's device 6502 (C,
   in `core/cpu6502/`) can later be cross-checked against it. The design's M3 6502 stays in
   M3. GoatTracker's source (GPL-2.0-or-later) is test-only and never linked into the
   firmware; SIDOU's player is written from the readme and `player.s`'s behaviour, credited,
   GPL-3.0-or-later (which "or later" permits).
4. **SID-Wizard import stays in M5,** as design section 14 already has it. M2 does not
   touch it. Reason: it needs its own player semantics, its documentation is thin (design
   section 18), and M2 is already large.
5. **Chip A plays the song; the keys play chip B** (design sections 1 and 5). With no song,
   chip A is silent and the keys sound as in M1, on chip B. *Open question for the user*
   (below): keep a KEYS A/B setting, default B.
6. **The store lives in [0x97000, 0xE0000)**, 292 KiB, the range the update loader never
   writes or erases and that the record scan skips (`ldr_core.c`); log-structured, CRC'd,
   page programs in short windows between audio blocks, erases only when the audio is quiet
   (M2a).
7. **The effect slots stay one chain, on the mix of both chips.** Design section 7 asked for
   slots per chip; a second chain's 128 KiB delay and reverb pool does not fit (see RAM
   below). The CHIP view's B tab shows chip B's profile and character, and the slots as
   shared.
8. **SCENE's vignettes are drawn pixel art,** composited per tile on demand (no frame
   buffer), with only original art; the prototype's drawing code survives in the published
   prototype artifact and is recovered as the art's source (M2f Task 1).

### Projected for M2 (estimates; each plan's first device task measures and records)

**Flash (image):**

| Plan | Adds (estimate) | Image after (cumulative) | Ceiling to assert |
|---|---|---|---|
| M2a store, settings, profiles | ~8 KB code | ~364 KB | 372,000 B |
| M2b chip B, expert layer, UI sounds | ~10 KB code | ~374 KB | 384,000 B |
| M2c format, player, extension, demo and About songs | ~28 KB (player ~6 KB, reader/writer ~8 KB, the rest) | ~402 KB | 414,000 B |

| M2f DUSK DUEL (optional) | ~22 KB | ~520 KB | 536,000 B |

That leaves **60-80 KB of the slot for M3** (the 6502 core, about 10-14 KB; PSID loading and
mute; the browser). M3's starter pack goes in the store, not the image. If flash runs short,
the levers are, in order: DUSK DUEL out; the art's ceiling; the 6581's WEAK and STRONG
combined waveforms (61,440 B, `SIDOU_ALL_COMBINED_PROFILES`; R2 and worn need them, see
docs/TODO.md). Each plan's packaging test asserts its ceiling (M2a Task 1 adds the test,
`flash_budget.rs`), so a plan that overshoots fails its own gate.

**RAMTEXT:** the player's tick, its command ring and the split render run in the interrupt:
about 5 KB (M2c Task 6, compiled `-Os` if it needs to be: it runs once a frame, not per
sample). The store's write windows reuse the flash driver already there. Projected
**~42 KB of 48**. M3's 6502 core (~8-12 KB) will not fit in what is left: M3 must grow
RAMTEXT into POOL (docs/hardware.md lists it) or compile the 6502 tightly. Flagged for M3.

**RAM (RAM region and POOL, 132 KB free together):**

| Item | Where | Bytes (estimate) |
|---|---|---|
| Chip B: a `sid` (~17.3 KB with its two filter rows), its DAC tables (2 × 17,408), ramp and stages | POOL | 52,700 (35,300 if A and B share one DAC build table, M2b Task 2 decides) |
| Song: content arena (patterns, orderlists) 32 KB, instruments and tables 3.7 KB, extension 4 KB, player state | POOL | 41,000 |
| Store: page buffer, sector index, file handles | RAM | 2,000 |
| Command and event rings (player), song edit queue | RAM | 3,000 |
| STEP, TRACKER and instrument page canvases | RAM | 6,000 |
| MIDI: clock follower, SysEx stream buffers | RAM | 2,000 |
| SCENE: RED MOON RITE state and tile hashes (900 × 4) | RAM | 5,000 |
| **Total** | | **~112 KB** (~94 KB with the shared DAC table) |

That leaves about 20-38 KB. **M3's 6502 needs the C64's 64 KB address space** in RAM for a
PSID: it cannot have it on top of M2. The plan's answer: M2c declares the song arena a
*content arena* that M3's 6502 memory will share (a song and a `.sid` never play at once),
sized 32 KB in M2 and grown in M3 together with one of: chip B's shared DAC table (17 KB),
a smaller slot pool (128 KiB of delay and reverb; 96 KiB caps the delay at about 0.74 s and
frees 32 KB), or a paged 6502 memory. **That choice is the user's, at M3's planning;** M2
must not spend the margin. Each plan's first device task reports the map's figures.

**Audio interrupt:** chip B is already inside the gates (they hold two chips' worst block,
1,426). M2 adds, always on: the second render call and the mix (~3 a sample), a quiet
detector for the store (~2), the command ring's pop (< 1). In song mode: the player's tick,
gated at **≤ 60 a sample in its worst block** (a tick of ~6k instructions in one 128-sample
block; M2c Task 6), and the frame split (a few a sample). Song mode and M3's 6502 never run
together, so **M3 keeps about 195-200 of today's 205** for the 6502 (docs/TODO.md, "The
6502 must fit the audio headroom", keeps the levers if it does not fit).

**Main loop:** every pass within `POLL_INSTRUCTIONS` (120k), and M2b Task 1 retires the one
exception (the chip reset) by preparing the spare chip over ticks. Saving, loading, parsing
a song, SysEx and the SCENE compositor all work in slices that keep each pass inside the
allowance; each plan's emu tests assert it.

**SPI:** every view holds to 200 cells a frame (17.5 ms with windows) as LIVE, SCENE and
CHIP do; RED MOON RITE's prototype measured 117 tiles a frame on average and 176 at most.

## Hardware flags (M2 items only a real FM-1 can settle)

- The flash part's program and erase times (`85 60 14`, a Puya 8 Mbit part; fm1-emu makes
  both instantaneous): whether a page program fits between two audio blocks, and how long a
  4 KiB erase holds the audio (M2a Task 4 measures with `fl_wait_ready`'s `took_us`).
- Whether erase-suspend (0x75/0x7A) works on that part (an option to keep the audio running
  through erases; not relied on).
- Whether masking interrupts for up to a millisecond right after an audio block ever drops
  a block (the DMA keeps playing the queued half meanwhile).
- That stock V15 and the SPL leave [0x97000, 0xE0000) alone (Felucca stores user data
  there, and its loader skips it), and that nothing in the store can look like an update
  record to the SPL (M2a Task 1 makes that impossible by construction, and tests it).
- Two SIDs and the player at 240 MHz against the instruction counts (the CPU clock is an
  open check in docs/hardware.md).
- MIDI clock out and SysEx replies over USB (fm1-emu has no USB).
- The LCD's look of RED MOON RITE's dither and colour ramps on the real panel.

## Risks and open questions (for the user's review)

- **KEYS A or B** (decision 5): the keys move from chip A to chip B, so with no song loaded
  M1's sound comes from chip B; the CHIP view's A tab then edits the song's chip. Proposed:
  default B, a KEYS setting (persisted) to put them back on A.
- **SAVE's button.** M1d gave SAVE to MIDI learn (press to arm). M2 needs a save. Proposed
  (M2a Task 5): SAVE **pressed and released** saves (song and settings, with the drive
  "chunk" if UI sounds are on); SAVE **held while a knob turns** arms learn (M1d's hold form,
  which already works); the learn frame's clear and forget gestures stay. To confirm.
- **Chip B song tracks** stay in M5 (design section 14). M2 stores chip B's *setup* in the
  extension and reserves the chunk for its tracks; STEP shows chip B's lane greyed.
- **Effect slots per chip** (design section 7) do not fit; M2 keeps one chain on the mix.
- **RAM for M3** (above): the content arena and the levers; the user decides at M3.
- **GoatTracker compatibility of the trailer.** GoatTracker 2.77's loader ignores trailing
  bytes; GoatTracker Stereo and forks that trial-read 6 orderlists must be checked too (M2c
  Task 1 lists the tools checked). If a tool rewrites the file, the trailer is gone and the
  song plays as a plain GT2 song: documented, not prevented.
- **Equivalence is per frame, not per cycle.** The test compares each frame's ordered SID
  writes; the C player cannot reproduce the 6502's write timing inside a frame. The device
  applies a frame's writes at the frame's sample. Documented in M2c Task 1.
- **RED MOON RITE's likeness.** It is a homage to late-80s fantasy sword-fighting games;
  the best known of them is a C64 classic with a barbarian duel. The art must not copy its
  sprites, poses, arena, colours, logo or its decapitation; M2f Task 1 adds a side-by-side
  originality review to the task, and the helmet knock is bloodless by design.
- **The prototype's code is not lost:** the published scene arcade artifact
  (<https://claude.ai/artifact/BEkze3t3jHNh9HCdXGPofP>, owned by the user) still carries
  RED MOON RITE's drawing code (`painter`, `puppet`, `barbSpec`, the poses, the arena). M2f
  Task 1 recovers it as `assets/scene/red-moon-rite.js` rather than re-deriving the art.
- **Design doc's tracker note.** Design section 7 did not say the tracker uses a moving bar
  by default; the prototype page did (and the user's brief). Section 7 is amended to say so.
