Songs to MIDI

Play it in the browser · Library · Songs for your FM-1 · Videos

Turn a GoatTracker 2 song into a Standard MIDI File: one MIDI channel for each SID voice, the notes where GoatTracker's player puts them. Play the file with any synth, or drop it into a DAW and give the voices new sounds. The song is read and converted in your browser; nothing is uploaded. It can come from a file, or straight from an FM-1 connected to your computer.

      What is in the file, and what is not

      • Voices: one track and one MIDI channel for each SID voice, channels 1 to 3 (1 to 6 for a GoatTracker Stereo song, whose second chip has voices 4 to 6; SIDOU's own chunk for a second chip's tracks, TRKB, is reserved and nothing writes it yet, so there is nothing of it to export). Every note has velocity 100: the SID has no velocity, and an instrument's volume is its envelope, which is not exported.
      • Pitch: a note is the row's note plus the orderlist's transpose, as the tracker shows it. An instrument's wavetable is followed: each step with a note (relative to the row's note, or a fixed one) that changes the pitch ends the sounding note and starts the next at the same tick, so an arpeggio is a run of short notes and a drum's pitch sweep a run of very short ones; a drum written as noise at a fixed pitch is the note it sounds, not the one on its row. The pattern commands that move the pitch (vibrato, slides, tone portamento) are not followed.
      • Timing: the player's own: speed (ticks a row), funktempo, tempo commands (the global and the voice's own), the orderlist's repeats and loop, and the gate timers. A note starts on the call where the player starts it and ends where the gate falls: on a key off, or one gate timer before the next note's row (GoatTracker's hard restart), so a note is a little shorter than its row. A legato instrument, a tone portamento or a wavetable step changes the pitch without a new gate: the file ends the old note and starts the new one at the same tick.
      • One pass: each voice plays its orderlist once and the file stops where it comes round to its loop point. A song with several subtunes has a menu; each is its own file.
      • Tempo: one tempo for the whole file, so that every note lands on a whole MIDI tick exactly (a MIDI tick is a twentieth of a player call, 1 ms at 50 Hz). A beat is the song's rows per beat (4, or what the song says) of its average row length: 6 ticks a row at 4 rows a beat is 125 BPM. A song that changes speed keeps its notes in time, but its rows no longer sit on the DAW's grid after the change; the changes are written as markers in the first track.
      • Not exported: the sounds (waveforms, envelopes, pulse width, filter), the chips' setup, the effect slots, the parameter locks and the rest of SIDOU's own settings. The file is the score of the song, not the sound.
